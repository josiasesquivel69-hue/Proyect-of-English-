HTML: the skeleton of every web page

By David Esquivel · Web development · Tutorial

Every website you visit starts with HTML. Think of it as the skeleton of a page: it gives structure to headings, paragraphs, images, and links. CSS then adds the style, and JavaScript adds the behavior. When I started learning web development, I thought HTML was too simple to matter. I was wrong. Good HTML makes a site easier to read, faster to load, and friendlier for people who use screen readers.

Here is a simple tip: always use semantic tags like header, nav, main, and footer instead of many div elements. They tell the browser, and search engines, what each part of the page means. Watch the short video below, then try building your own small page with these tags today.

Why I made this video

I created this short video myself with an AI video tool because I wanted a clear visual for my post. In only five seconds, it shows a web page as a skeleton and names each part with a tag: header, nav, main, and footer. Beginners often feel lost when they see many tags, so a quick image helps them understand the big picture first. The video complements my written post because I explain why HTML matters and give one practical tip, while the video shows the structure on the screen. Reading and watching together helps different learning styles. Because it is short and simple, it keeps the audience engaged, and readers can open their editor and practice immediately.
